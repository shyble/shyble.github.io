*I'm building Noetic, and I'm looking for the first people to build it with.*

## One question

Today's best models reason step by step, write code and pass professional exams. Ask one *why* it decided, and you get a fluent story. Ask it where that story stops being true, and it has nothing to say.

So here is the question I've spent this year on:

**What would it take for an AI to know what it actually understands?**

Not to sound confident. Not to explain itself well. To *know*, and to tell you honestly when it doesn't.

## The questions behind it

<img src="/images/noetic-nodes.png" alt="" style="float: right; width: 34%; max-width: 220px; margin: 0.25rem 0 1rem 1.5rem; border-radius: 12px;">

- Can a model tell the knowledge it understands from the knowledge it merely uses?
- Can it know where its own answer stops being valid, before it's wrong?
- Can it notice that the world has changed, before you do?
- Can its decisions be checked by someone who doesn't trust it?

I think the answer to all four can be yes. Noetic is my answer.
